Research design · hypotheses

Five consistency checks around a frozen detector

Every design keeps the existing detector unchanged, adds one cheap physical cue, and turns their disagreement into a calibrated decision that can also say "not enough evidence".

InputCamera frames with or without a physical attack
→
Existing detectorFrozen object or sign detector: boxes, labels, confidence
+
Independent cueCheap physical evidence: shape, shadow, reflection, light, motion
→
Consistency checkDo the cue and the detector output agree?
→
DecisionFlag with the region and cue that caused it, or no alarm or abstain
Shared evaluation stance: compare methods at matched false-alarm operating points, on held-out recordings, and report processing time per frame. None of these designs has been implemented or tested yet.
1. Object appearance

Viewpoint-aware structural disagreement

Low-cost evidence that an object's geometry and the detector's output disagree, without rebuilding the whole image.

Independent cue
Contour, edges, sign shape, local colour and texture of the object region
Consistency check
Align the object region, track viewpoint and scale, compare the contour with the box and the label
Proposed module
Viewpoint-conditioned shape and detector mismatch with calibrated uncertainty
Open questions
  • Does class-specific contour consistency add signal against natural-looking patches, beyond texture?
  • Can a tiny head generalize across viewing angles?
Known weak point. Severe occlusion or artistic signs may destroy the geometric evidence; abstentions must be reported.

2. Shadows

Physics-gated temporal shadow evidence

Check whether shadow movement is physically plausible before treating a detector failure as an attack.

Independent cue
Shadow mask and edges, motion and a light-direction proxy
Consistency check
Align the shadow to the sign, correct for camera motion, estimate the plausible change
Proposed module
Shadow and geometry consistency residual with a reliability gate
Open questions
  • Can a physical reliability gate lower false positives on tree and cloud shadows?
  • Can it detect temporally coherent attack shadows?

Closest prior work flagged by the literature pass (not yet audited):

  • Exposing photo manipulation with inconsistent shadows (Kee, 2013)
  • Validating the Contextual Information of Outdoor Images for Photo Misuse Detection (Li, 2018)
  • Shadows Aren't So Dangerous After All: A Fast and Robust Defense Against Shadow-Based Adversarial Attacks (Wang, 2022)
Known weak point. Physically plausible malicious shadows may be indistinguishable from benign ones in RGB alone.

3. Reflections

View-conditioned light consistency

Detect suspicious object responses to changing view and illumination without assuming every reflection is malicious.

Independent cue
Specular regions and saturation, camera motion and a light proxy
Consistency check
Track the reflected region, contrast it with the nearby surface, estimate exposure drift
Proposed module
View-conditioned reflection residual with a confidence or abstain output
Open questions
  • Do low-cost view and illumination constraints reject natural highlights while detecting visible reflected-light attacks?

Closest prior work flagged by the literature pass (not yet audited):

  • Remote Perception Attacks against Camera-based Object Recognition Systems and Countermeasures (Man, 2024)
  • Invisible Reflections: Leveraging Infrared Laser Reflections to Target Traffic Sign Perception (Sato, 2024)
Known weak point. An RGB camera with an infrared-cut filter may not see an infrared attack; sensor assumptions must be stated.

4. Lighting

Global-local exposure and flicker test

Combine local-versus-global illumination residuals with temporal flicker patterns under a fixed compute budget.

Independent cue
Global luminance trend, local residuals and stripe cues
Consistency check
Normalize exposure drift, track local light change, estimate flicker consistency
Proposed module
Exposure-aware local light anomaly score with a temporal flicker gate
Open questions
  • Can a tiny exposure-flicker detector identify projected attacks without flagging normal headlight and auto-exposure changes?

Closest prior work flagged by the literature pass (not yet audited):

  • Remote Perception Attacks against Camera-based Object Recognition Systems and Countermeasures (Man, 2024)
  • They See Me Rollin’: Inherent Vulnerability of the Rolling Shutter in CMOS Image Sensors (Köhler, 2021)
  • Invisible Adversarial Stripes on Traffic Sign: Threat and Defense for Autonomous Vehicles (Guo, 2026)
Known weak point. Rolling-shutter and frame-rate aliasing can hide or imitate flicker; tests must cover several sensors.

5. Changes across video frames

Bounded causal multi-cue evidence

Lightweight sequential disagreement scoring with measured latency and an explicit insufficient-evidence state.

Independent cue
Optical flow, local light and appearance consistency across recent frames
Consistency check
Align recent frames, extract trajectory and cue gaps, check quality and occlusion
Proposed module
Causal evidence accumulation with bounded frame memory
Open questions
  • Can bounded multi-cue evidence improve recall or warning time for unseen attacks while keeping a fixed false-alarm rate?

Closest prior work flagged by the literature pass (not yet audited):

  • AdROD: HyperNetwork-based Adversarially Robust Object Detection for Autonomous Driving (Wu, 2026)
  • VisionGuard: Secure and Robust Visual Perception of Autonomous Vehicles in Practice (Han, 2024)
  • A Real-Time Defense Against Object Vanishing Adversarial Patch Attacks for Object Detection in Autonomous Vehicles (Mu, 2025)
Known weak point. Prior work already uses temporal and physical consistency; any gain has to be shown as a controlled increment.
