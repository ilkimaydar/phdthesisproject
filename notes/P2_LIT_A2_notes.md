## 1. Findings first (uncomfortable ones first)
1. Prior work already covers large parts of the slide 02 module (details in section 4): a dedicated defense against the shadow attack (P2-P208), a training-time defense with no added run-time cost (P2-P209), temporal and historical comparison defenses for sign attacks (P2-P210, P2-P211), a geometric shadow-consistency test with a feasible or infeasible verdict (P2-P228), a sun-position residual computed from one shadow against astronomical sun position (P2-P230), shadows as physical invariants for detecting perception attacks in LiDAR (P2-P234, P2-P235), and cross-frame shadow consistency inside video shadow detectors (P2-P213, P2-P214, P2-P217, P2-P219).
2. The shadow attack of P2-P201 uses natural shadows. A crafted shadow cast by a real occluder is a physically valid shadow, so a plausibility check can only help where the crafted shadow breaks scene physics (occluder not in the scene, shadow disagrees with the other shadows or with the sun, shadow stops at the sign boundary). That is a hypothesis to test, and it is the slide's own critical failure case.
3. To our knowledge, within the records found, no work checks the temporal physical plausibility of a camera shadow (light or sun direction plus camera motion) to flag attacks on sign recognition. This is a search result under tool limits, not proof of absence.
4. In the records found, shadow-attack literature for cameras is small: one CVPR 2022 paper (P2-P201), two 2025 papers whose abstracts were not read (P2-P202, P2-P203), one diffusion-based shadow attack (P2-P205, abstract unread), and a sunlight-shadow attack on lane detection (P2-P204). The one dedicated shadow defense found is a preprint (P2-P208).
5. No real road-scene video dataset with shadow masks was found. Road-related shadow data found is synthetic (P2-P240, P2-P241) or a relighting method (P2-P242). Video shadow data is general-scene (ViSha, P2-P236).
6. No CPU timing was found for any shadow detector. Speed figures are GPU or unstated (P2-P221: 32 FPS, hardware not stated; P2-P223: 66 FPS on an Nvidia 2080Ti; P2-P218: real-time claim without hardware).

## 4. Closest prior work to slide 02 ("shadow/geometry consistency residual plus a reliability gate"), ranked
1. P2-P228 (Kee, O'Brien, Farid, ACM TOG 2013; abstract read via Crossref). Poses consistency of cast and attached shadows as a linear program on the projected light position; a feasible solution means physically plausible, no solution is evidence of tampering. Covers the geometry-consistency idea. Not covered: video, camera motion, detector failures, an abstain gate; robustness to noisy masks not stated in the abstract.
2. P2-P230 (Li 2018, preprint). Infers sun position from one shadow, compares with astronomical sun position for a claimed time and place, reports thresholds in degrees. Closest "residual against a physical model" with a security purpose. Photos, not video; not aimed at detector failures.
3. P2-P210 (Etim 2024, preprint). Defense for sticker, light and shadow attacks on sign classification by voting over past images of the same sign. Closest temporal defense for sign attacks; no light physics; depends on historical imagery.
4. P2-P208 (Wang 2022, preprint). The only defense found that is built for the shadow attack; input-side. It is the baseline any shadow module must beat. No physical plausibility test, no time.
5. P2-P209 (LAMDA 2026, preprint). Training-time, no run-time cost, evaluated on shadow, natural-light and patch attacks. A strong zero-latency comparator.
6. P2-P235 and P2-P234 (Hau 2022 and 2021). Shadows as a physical invariant to detect object-hiding and ghost-object attacks. Same logic as slide 02 (a missing object leaves a shadow), but LiDAR voids rather than camera shadows. P2-P234 is metadata-only.
7. P2-P211 (Joshi 2025, preprint). Sequence voting plus entropy anomaly detection for sign recognition under glare, rain and attacks; a temporal baseline without shadow physics.
8. P2-P219, P2-P213, P2-P214, P2-P217. Video shadow detectors with cross-frame consistency; P2-P219 pairs each shadow with the casting object and tracks the pair. These supply the cue, not an attack decision.
9. P2-P202 (CVPRW 2025). The temporal attack the module must face; it may also contain a defense (abstract unread).

## 5. Answers to the four questions of the brief
1. Physical-world and video shadow attacks. P2-P201: real-world tests with a moving camera against sign recognition (models not named in the abstract). P2-P204: physical testbed and controlled field evaluation against lane detection. Video: the moving-camera result of P2-P201; the title of P2-P202 states sign sequences. No record read describes a physical shadow attack on a general object detector.
2. Defenses and cost: section 3 table. Training-time: P2-P209 (no run-time cost per the abstract). Input transformation: P2-P208. Detection or voting: P2-P210, P2-P211. No run-time figure in milliseconds appears in any abstract read.
3. Physical plausibility of a shadow over time for security: not found for camera video. Related pieces exist for single images (P2-P228, P2-P230, P2-P227, P2-P229), for LiDAR (P2-P234, P2-P235) and for non-physical temporal voting (P2-P210, P2-P211).
4. CPU-first shadow detectors: none has a CPU timing in its abstract. Candidates by design: classical chromacity, geometry and texture cues (P2-P224 abstract: the chromacity method is the fastest to implement and run but noise-sensitive; P2-P245 title only), an SVM prior plus a patch-level CNN (P2-P222: one to two orders of magnitude lower time cost than then state of the art, hardware not stated), SDDNet (P2-P221, 32 FPS, hardware not stated). Natural shadows on road scenes: only synthetic sets (P2-P240 over 50K images, P2-P241 90,292 images and 1,596 clips) and a relighting method (P2-P242); general-scene video shadows in ViSha (P2-P236).

## 6. Gaps and what is left to test
1. Whether a shadow consistency residual (shadow on the sign versus other shadows in the frame versus expected sun direction) separates crafted shadows from tree, cloud and building shadows. No record tests this.
2. Whether physically valid crafted shadows can pass the residual (finding 2). Needs an attack with a visible or hidden occluder and a matched benign set.
3. Reliability gate: when shadow masks, alignment or sun estimates are unreliable, how often the system must abstain. No record gives abstention rates for shadow-based checks.
4. Camera-motion compensation for shadow evidence over time: optical-flow warping exists inside a shadow detector (P2-P217); its use in an attack decision is untested.
5. CPU latency of shadow cue extractors on road frames.
6. Data: attack sequences are not available as a downloadable set in this catalog; the code of P2-P201 was not located; benign-shadow hard negatives with labels are missing.
7. Published versions of arXiv-only rows were not checked (P2-P204, P2-P208 to P2-P211, P2-P216 to P2-P218, P2-P220, P2-P222, P2-P223, P2-P225, P2-P226, P2-P230, P2-P237 to P2-P242).

## 10. Problem cards (house layout; each card states a hypothesis, never a result)
Laptop limits from state/DECK_DIGEST.md section 4: one laptop, 32 GB RAM, several TB SSD, GPU and VRAM unknown; CPU baselines and cached features first; expand only after a measured fit test; record each licence before download or release; split by recording, never by neighbouring frames.

### Card A2-1: Crafted shadows pass the detector, and the defenses found do not test physics
- Problem: a shadow shaped by an attacker can flip sign recognition (P2-P201); the defenses found change the input or the training and do not ask whether the shadow is physically consistent.
- Contribution to test (hypothesis): a shadow-consistency residual adds recall at a fixed false-alarm rate beyond detector-only and beyond the input transform of P2-P208. Prior work already covers: input transform (P2-P208), training-time robustness (P2-P209), historical and sequence voting (P2-P210, P2-P211), a still-image shadow geometry test (P2-P228). Not found: a residual plus gate on camera video against sign attacks.
- Open-source starting point: OpenCV shadow-cue features and scikit-learn (named on the deck); classical cue families (P2-P224); code link of P2-P208 (stated in its abstract, location not seen); attack-generation code for P2-P201 not located (NEEDS_VERIFY).
- Data and laptop fit: LISA and GTSRB (P2-P201, P2-P208, P2-P209 use them); crafted-shadow sequences must be rendered or recorded; CPU only with cached features. Licence question: LISA, GTSRB and any attacked images derived from them (redistribution) are unchecked.
- Experiment workflow: (1) train or load a frozen sign classifier on GTSRB and LISA; (2) generate polygonal shadow attacks and natural shadows on the same signs; (3) compute shadow masks and consistency features, fit logistic regression or boosting on cached features; (4) compare detector-only, P2-P208 transform, shadow-only and combined on held-out recordings at matched false alarms.
- Success measure: recall at a fixed false-alarm rate, false alarms per hour on ordinary driving clips, milliseconds per frame, share of abstained cases.
- Sources: P2-P201, P2-P208, P2-P209, P2-P210, P2-P228, P2-P224.

### Card A2-2: Shadow motion over frames must agree with camera motion and light
- Problem: in video a crafted shadow persists (P2-P201 moving-camera result; P2-P202 title), while benign tree, cloud and building shadows move and deform with ego-motion and scene geometry; shadow masks themselves flicker between frames (P2-P214).
- Contribution to test (hypothesis): a motion-compensated temporal consistency score of the shadow region on the sign, with a gate that abstains when alignment fails, separates coherent crafted shadows from benign moving shadows. Prior work already covers: cross-frame consistency inside shadow detectors (P2-P213, P2-P214, P2-P217) and shadow-object tracking (P2-P219). Not found: use in an attack decision.
- Open-source starting point: OpenCV optical flow and ECC alignment (named on the deck); mask sources with code named in abstracts: STICT (P2-P213), Detect-Any-Shadow (P2-P216), DTTNet (P2-P218), all unopened.
- Data and laptop fit: ViSha for mask quality (P2-P236, general scenes); BDD100K driving clips named on the deck for benign shadows; attack clips to be generated. CPU baseline with classical masks and flow; deep masks only after a fit test. Licence question: ViSha and BDD100K terms unchecked.
- Experiment workflow: (1) cache sign-region shadow masks per clip with one classical and one small deep extractor; (2) estimate global motion per frame pair and warp the previous mask; (3) compute residuals (mask overlap, intensity-profile change) with a reliability weight from alignment error; (4) evaluate temporal-only, shadow-only and combined scores, and report abstentions.
- Success measure: recall at matched false-alarm rate, false alarms per hour, p95 milliseconds per frame including alignment, abstention share.
- Sources: P2-P201, P2-P202, P2-P213, P2-P214, P2-P217, P2-P219.

### Card A2-3: Light direction and sun position as a checkable prior
- Problem: shadows in a scene must agree with the dominant light; in daylight the sun direction can be computed from time and place (used for photos in P2-P230), but no record applies this to attacks on sign recognition.
- Contribution to test (hypothesis): the angle between the shadow-implied light direction and the expected sun azimuth, or between shadows in the same frame, stays small for natural scenes and grows for shadows from an occluder outside the scene, gated by estimate uncertainty (cloud cover, few usable shadows). Prior work already covers: shadow-geometry test on stills (P2-P228), one-shadow sun position against astronomy on photos (P2-P230), learned sun estimates (P2-P232). Not found: video, vehicle motion, sign attacks.
- Open-source starting point: the solar position algorithm of P2-P233 (an open implementation; library choice UNVERIFIED); the linear-program idea of P2-P228 (code not seen); the CNN of P2-P232 as a heavier reference; OpenCV line fitting on pole and sign shadows.
- Data and laptop fit: needs time and location per clip. Whether BDD100K recordings carry usable time and GPS is UNVERIFIED. Geometry runs on CPU; the CNN only after a fit test. Licence question: dataset terms unchecked.
- Experiment workflow: (1) select clips with known time and place and visible shadow-casting objects; (2) extract object-shadow line pairs; (3) compute the residual against sun azimuth and against other shadows; (4) test on benign tree, cloud and building shadows against rendered crafted shadows, and count abstentions.
- Success measure: separation of benign and crafted shadows by the residual alone (recall at matched false alarms), error in degrees (P2-P230 reports thresholds of 9.4 and 5 degrees on its photos as a reference scale), abstention share.
- Sources: P2-P228, P2-P230, P2-P232, P2-P233, P2-P231, P2-P229.

### Card A2-4: A shadow cue extractor small enough for a laptop CPU
- Problem: video shadow detectors are mostly deep networks with GPU or unstated speed figures (P2-P221, P2-P223, P2-P218); CPU cost is unknown.
- Contribution to test (hypothesis): a classical prior plus a small model gives masks accurate enough on the sign region to feed the consistency check at a fraction of the cost of a deep detector. Prior work already covers: fast detectors (P2-P222, P2-P221) and classical cue families (P2-P224, P2-P245). Not found: CPU timings on road frames.
- Open-source starting point: OpenCV classical cues; code named in abstracts for P2-P213, P2-P216, P2-P218, P2-P220 (unopened).
- Data and laptop fit: ViSha (P2-P236), the 10,500-mask set (P2-P237) and instance data (P2-P238) for masks; no road-specific shadow masks. CPU first; ONNX export only after measurement (deck rule). Licence question: all three sets unchecked.
- Experiment workflow: (1) fix three extractors: classical chromacity and geometry, a patch-level small network, one pre-trained deep model; (2) measure overlap on shadows touching sign regions and on whole frames; (3) measure p50 and p95 milliseconds per frame and peak memory on the laptop CPU; (4) choose the accuracy-latency trade-off point.
- Success measure: balanced error or overlap on sign-region shadows against p95 milliseconds per frame and peak memory.
- Sources: P2-P222, P2-P221, P2-P218, P2-P224, P2-P245, P2-P236, P2-P237.

### Card A2-5: Benign shadows cause the false alarms, and road data with shadow labels is thin
- Problem: natural shadows degrade AV perception (P2-P241: shadows are the most disruptive of 14 illusions for lane detectors; P2-P242: cast shadows lower DNN accuracy in urban driving scenes), so a check for crafted shadows meets many benign ones.
- Contribution to test (hypothesis): a hard-negative set built from synthetic shadows (P2-P239 recipe), road relighting (P2-P242), CARLA illusions (P2-P241) and real clips lowers the false-alarm rate of the check more than adding attack positives. Prior work already covers: synthesis methods and one benchmark exist. Not found: a sign-focused benign-shadow hard-negative set.
- Open-source starting point: SynShadow code (P2-P239, named in abstract), CARLA (named in P2-P241), LightCity (P2-P240).
- Data and laptop fit: LanEvil++ and LightCity are synthetic and serve lane and inverse-rendering tasks, not sign recognition; real clips from BDD100K named on the deck; download sizes not stated in the records. Licence question: LanEvil++, LightCity, ViSha and BDD100K terms unchecked.
- Experiment workflow: (1) define a shadow taxonomy: tree, cloud, building, vehicle, pole, crafted; (2) build matched benign and attack clips per sign; (3) mine hard negatives from the false alarms of cards A2-1 to A2-3; (4) measure false alarms per hour on long negative clips.
- Success measure: false alarms per hour at matched recall, broken down by shadow type.
- Sources: P2-P241, P2-P240, P2-P239, P2-P242, P2-P236, P2-P244.
